What an EOB is

An EOB is not a bill — it's a statement showing how your insurance processed a claim. Wait for the actual bill from the provider before paying — and compare it to the EOB.

An Explanation of Benefits (EOB) is a statement from your insurer explaining:

  • What the provider charged
  • What your plan allowed (negotiated rate)
  • What your insurance paid
  • What you may owe (deductible, copay, coinsurance)

An EOB is not a bill. Wait for the actual bill from the provider before paying — and compare it to the EOB.

EOB checklist — go line by line

# Check item What to look for Yes / No / N/A
1Provider name matches who treated you
2Date of service is correct
3Service / procedure code matches what you received
4Billed amount matches what you were told
5Allowed amount (negotiated rate) is shown
6Insurance paid amount is shown
7Your responsibility (deductible / copay / coinsurance) is shown
8Deductible applied correctly (have you met it?)
9Copay amount matches your plan
10Coinsurance % matches your plan
11Claim was not denied without reason
12Denial reason is explained (if denied)
13No duplicate charges for same service/date
14You received an actual bill from the provider
15Provider bill matches EOB "you may owe" amount

Common red flags

  • Billed amount far above the allowed amount (balance billing may apply depending on network status and state law)
  • Service you didn't receive
  • Duplicate charges
  • Denial with no clear reason
  • Out-of-network charges when you believed the provider was in-network

What to do if something looks wrong

  • Call the provider's billing office and ask for an itemized bill.
  • Call your insurer's member services (number on your card) and ask them to explain the claim.
  • If the insurer denies a service you believe should be covered, use the Denial Letter Tracker and Appeal Letter Templates in this kit.
  • Take notes: date, time, who you spoke with, reference numbers.
  • Keep the EOB and any bills in your records.

Key terms

  • Allowed amount: The maximum amount your plan will pay for a covered service (the negotiated rate with in-network providers).
  • Deductible: The amount you pay out-of-pocket before your plan starts paying.
  • Copay: A fixed amount you pay for a service (e.g., $25 for an office visit).
  • Coinsurance: The percentage you pay after meeting your deductible (e.g., 20%).
  • Balance billing: When a provider bills you for the difference between their charge and the allowed amount. Rules vary by network status and state law.
