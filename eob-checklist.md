# Explanation of Benefits (EOB) Checklist

Walk through every line of an EOB before you pay a medical bill. An EOB is not a bill — it's a statement showing how your insurance processed a claim. Education only — not legal advice.

## What an EOB is

An Explanation of Benefits (EOB) is a statement from your insurer explaining:
- What the provider charged
- What your plan allowed (negotiated rate)
- What your insurance paid
- What you may owe (deductible, copay, coinsurance)

An EOB is not a bill. Wait for the actual bill from the provider before paying — and compare it to the EOB.

## Common red flags

- Billed amount far above the allowed amount (balance billing may apply depending on network status and state law)
- Service you didn't receive
- Duplicate charges
- Denial with no clear reason
- Out-of-network charges when you believed the provider was in-network

## What to do if something looks wrong

1. Call the provider's billing office and ask for an itemized bill.
2. Call your insurer's member services (number on your card) and ask them to explain the claim.
3. If the insurer denies a service you believe should be covered, use the Denial Letter Tracker and Appeal Letter Templates in this kit.
4. Take notes: date, time, who you spoke with, reference numbers.
5. Keep the EOB and any bills in your records.

## Key terms

- **Allowed amount**: The maximum amount your plan will pay for a covered service (the negotiated rate with in-network providers).
- **Deductible**: The amount you pay out-of-pocket before your plan starts paying.
- **Copay**: A fixed amount you pay for a service (e.g., $25 for an office visit).
- **Coinsurance**: The percentage you pay after meeting your deductible (e.g., 20%).
- **Balance billing**: When a provider bills you for the difference between their charge and the allowed amount. Rules vary by network status and state law.
